Radiative Transfer

Background

The radiative transfer method is based on a moment method from Hannah Jang-Condell. DustRad computes the heating at the disk midplane due to starlight intercepted at the disk's starlight-absorbing surface. Instead of solving a full 3D, wavelength-dependent radiative transfer, it:

  • Reconstructs an analytic vertical density profile from 2D (vertically integrated) quantities and locates the starlight-absorbing surface (the τ≈2/3 surface).
  • Uses a plane-parallel analytic expression for the intensity received from each small surface patch (an expression of the form \( B(\tau_s,\mu) \)).
  • Integrates (sums) contributions of many surface patches to obtain the bolometric intensity at each midplane point, accounting for shadowing by the disk photosphere.
  • Converts that intensity into a net heating rate and applies it in FARGO3D's source step, updating internal energy and scale height.

Finding the Surface

DustRad makes several key assumptions to solve for the starlight-absorbing surface from the 2D, vertically-averaged density distribution used for the hydrodynamics.

Two-temperature, gray approximation: DustRad treats the disk as a two-temperature vertical profile (warm surface, cooler midplane) and uses gray opacities (separate gray opacity regimes for stellar and thermal wavelengths). The surface temperature is set by local balance of starlight absorption and radiative cooling.

Surface temperature: the surface temperature used for the atmosphere is

$$T_{\rm surf}(r) = \epsilon_s^{-1/4}\sqrt{\dfrac{R_*}{2r}}\,T_* $$

(Equation 13 in the paper). Here \( \epsilon_s = \kappa_{P}/\kappa^*_{P} \) is the emissivity ratio (Planck opacities for disk vs stellar spectrum).

Piecewise vertical density: The vertical distribution is assumed to be Gaussian-like layers with different scale heights above and below the photosphere height \(z_{ph}\):

$$ \rho_{d,0}(r,\phi,z) = \begin{cases} \rho_{\rm mid} \exp\!\big(-z^2/(2H_{\rm mid}^2)\big), & z \le z_{ph} \\[0.3em] \rho_{\rm ph} \exp\!\big(-z^2/(2H_{\rm ph}^2)\big), & z > z_{ph} \end{cases} $$

Using the surface information, the radiation from each element is calculated and the contribution at the midplane is summed. The calculations are as follows

Intensity from a surface element (local, plane-parallel form)

$$ B(\tau_s,\mu) = \frac{\sigma_B T^4}{\pi} = \frac{\alpha_{\rm abs} F_{\rm irr}\mu}{4\pi}\big(c_1 + c_2 e^{-\tau_s} + c_3 e^{-\beta\mu\tau_s}\big) $$

In DustRad the non-diffuse terms are negligible at the midplane, so DustRad simplifies this to the diffuse term

$$ B(\tau_s,\mu) \;\approx\; \frac{\alpha_{\rm abs} F_{\rm irr}\mu}{4\pi}\,c_1 $$

Here \(F_{\rm irr}\) is the stellar flux incident on that surface patch and \(\mu\) the cosine of local incidence angle.

Sum Intensity from Surface

$$ B_{\rm tot}(P) \simeq \frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{n} B_i(\tau_i,\mu_i)\,\nu_i\,\Delta\Omega_i $$

Each surface element contributes its intensity over the solid angle \(\Delta\Omega_i\) as seen from midplane point \(P\). The geometric factor \(\nu_i\) accounts for projected area/orientation.

Heating & cooling -> source-term update

$$ Q_{\rm net} = Q_{\rm rad} - Q_{\rm cool} \quad,\quad Q_{\rm rad} = \dfrac{\pi B_{\rm tot}}{\tau_{\rm eff}} $$ $$ \tau_{\rm eff} = \dfrac{3\tau}{8} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4\tau},\quad \tau = \kappa_P \Sigma_{d,1}/2 $$ $$ Q_{\rm cool} = \dfrac{\sigma T^4}{\tau_{\rm eff}} $$

Update energy: \( e_t = e_{t-\Delta t} + Q_{\rm net}\Delta t \). Update sound speed and relax scale height toward hydrostatic equilibrium.

Speed-Ups to Calculation

The analytic moment method offers a fast calculation of the heating from radiative transfer. The method has been streamlined from Jang-Condell 2008 since we are only solving for the heating at the midplane. Even with this refinement, radiative transfer is the slowest step in DustRad if done at the same resolution as the hydrodynamics. Therefore, we introduce dedicated grids for radiative transfer that can be much coarser than the hydro grid:

  • Separate RT grids:
    • Surface grid (for star-irradiated surface regions)
    • Midplane grid (for heating points)
    Both can use fewer points in \(r\) and \(\phi\) than the hydro grid. The computed heating field is interpolated back onto the hydro grid afterward.
  • Center sampling of surface regions: Each surface region initially uses its geometric center to represent the entire area, so one ray and one solid angle calculation replaces many.
  • Adaptive subdivision: If the center cell of a surface region contributes a significant portion of the midplane intensity, it is sampled more finely to more accurately capture the flux contributed by the region. The limits of this this adaptive flux calcuation are determined by the user inputs \(f_1\) and \(f_2\). These inputs are represent the ratio of the flux for a surface region to the maximum flux calculated. If the initial flux calculation from the center cell of the surface region is below the lower limit, \(f_1\), then just the center cell is used. If it is above the upper limit, \(f_2\), then every cell in the region is sampled. If it is inbetween, then an additional fraction of the cells \( F \) are calculated according to the following formula: $$F = \ln{(f/f_2)}/\ln{(f_1/f_2)}$$
  • Photosphere shadowing accelerates too: The shadow test not only improves realism (no double-heating of shadowed zones) but also avoids calculating contributions from surface cells that are fully blocked.

Setup

The .opt file

To include the heating from radiative transfer in simulations, add the following line: 

    FFARGO_OPT += -DRUNRADTRANS

The heating at the midplane is calculated from the radiation each midplane point receives from the surface points. We do not consider the diffusion of radiation between points along the midplane. Instead, we utilize the adiabatic equation of state in FARGO3D to allow for advection and diffusion of heat via the gas motion. 

    FARGO_OPT += -DADIABATIC

The .par file

The radiative transfer calculations requires information on the disk opacity. These are specifified by the following parameters in the .par file:

  • Rosseland: The Rosseland mean opacity of the small dust grains averaged over the disk thermal wavelength spectrum
  • Planck: The Planck mean opacity of the small dust grains averaged over the stellar wavelength spectrum
  • RatioPNum: The average absorption of the small dust over the disk thermal wavelength spectrum. Used in the ratio of absorption to extinction.
  • AbsFracNum:The average absorption of the small dust over the stellar wavelength spectrum. Used in the ratio of absorption to extinction.

We have included the option for users to specify resolutions for the radiative transfer calculations that are separate from the hydrodynamic grid. The surface and midplane resolutions can be given in both the radial and azimuthal directions. The following variables should be specified in the .par file:

  • Nx_rad: Number of azimuthal points in midplane for which heating is calculated
  • Ny_rad: Number of radial points in midplane for which heating is calculated
  • NxRadSurf: Number of azimuthal surface points from which radiation is received
  • NyRadSurf: Number of radial surface points from which radiation is received

Implementation

The sequence of calculations for the radiative transfer occur in the file Rad_main.c. The primary steps in the calculation can be summarized as:

  • Transpose Domain Decompositon: The initial domain decomposition from FARGO3D's hydrodynamics separates the disk into annular rings that are treated separately by each processor used in the parallel computation. This decomposition benefits FARGO3D's fast orbital advection scheme, but is not ideal for our radiative transfer calculations. The starlight absorbing surface must be solved from the inner radial boundary to the outer radial boundary. So our first step is to redo the domain decompositon into radial strips using MPI communication. This occurs in rad_transfer_cpu() in Rad_main.c.
  • Determine Starlight-Absorbing Surface: The height of the surface is calculated in calc_surf_twotemp() in the file Rad_surface.c. The disk conditions at the inner radial boundary are extrapolated inward to determine the starting surface height at the inner boundary. Then the optical depth is calculated on rays extending radially outward using the volume density and the opacity of the small dust grain population. The exact height of the starlight absorbing surface (\( \tau=2/3 \)) is interpolated from the value of these rays at each point in the hydrodynamic grid.
  • Communicate Surface Between Processors: Each processor then needs the surface information to calculate the radiation received by each midplane point (within their separate domains) from each surface point (within and beyond their domain). This communication occurs in rad_transfer_cpu() in Rad_main.c.
  • Calculate Radiation from Surface: The contribution from each surface element to each midplane element is calculated in B_flux_azi() and then summed in calc_flux_azi(), both of which are found in Rad_flux.c.
  • Update Energy and Scale Height: The cells are returned to their original domain decomposition and the heating is added to the cells in update_temp_height() in Rad_main.c. The sound speed is updated and the scale height is relaxed toward the new hydrostatic equilibrium over an orbital timescale.
